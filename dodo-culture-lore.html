Explore how the dodo shaped culture and inspired lore

The dodo closed its eyes for the last time only 100 years after its discovery, but it lived on in our imaginations. Misunderstood more than any other bird, the dodo became an icon of extinction and humans’ capacity to change the world forever. From its origin on the isolated island of Mauritius to absurd court paintings in the 1600s and its legendary place in Alice In Wonderland, the dodo has covered a lot of ground from reality into fiction, and back.

OUTSIZED TALES

The dodo is embedded in culture and memory, but how much of it is true?

From wild stories to immortality

For many people, their introduction to the mysterious creature came through pop culture, not ecology or zoology. Exaggerated representations in art and fiction shaped this flightless bird’s story through the decades — the truth of the dodo took flight, until now.

+

Beneath the caricature lies a real animal, shaped by time, isolation, and a fragile island ecosystem.

FACT

transforming the animal into something closer to fantasy than reality.

FACT

The dodo thrived in its native Mauritian forests, well-adapted to hunt and forage.

More friendly than foolish, the dodo didn’t fear the new settlers because it lived on an island with no natural predators.

Similar to its living relative the pigeon, it was reasonably brainy for a bird of its size.
