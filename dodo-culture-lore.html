Explore how the dodo shaped culture and inspired lore

The dodo closed its eyes for the last time only 100 years after its discovery, but it lived on in our imaginations. Misunderstood more than any other bird, the dodo became an icon of extinction and humans’ capacity to change the world forever. From its origin on the isolated island of Mauritius to absurd court paintings in the 1600s and its legendary place in Alice In Wonderland, the dodo has covered a lot of ground from reality into fiction, and back.

OUTSIZED TALES

The dodo is embedded in culture and memory, but how much of it is true?

From wild stories to immortality

For many people, their introduction to the mysterious creature came through pop culture, not ecology or zoology. Exaggerated representations in art and fiction shaped this flightless bird’s story through the decades — the truth of the dodo took flight, until now.

+

Beneath the caricature lies a real animal, shaped by time, isolation, and a fragile island ecosystem.

Displays & Curiosities

As the public heard tales of the dodo, fascination grew. Live dodos were transported from Mauritius as exotic curiosities for wealthy collectors and menageries, and lifelike models were made with real bones for museums.

+
A dodo was recorded at the menagerie of the Mogul Emperor Jahangir in about 1625, and in about 1638, Sir Hamon L'Estrange saw a live dodo exhibited in London. Stories powered the bird's journey across continents and cultures, but it still carried its distorted reputation no matter how long it traveled.
Joris Hoefnagl
EARLY 1600s

FIRSTHAND DRAWINGS BY SAILORS

Dutch sailors on trading voyages provided some of the most realistic accounts of the dodo. One skilled artist on board was Jooris Joostensz Laerle who created several drawings that depicted thinner, active birds.

ROELAND SAVERY
C. 1626

The Savery paintings

More famous than scientifically accurate, Dutch artist Roelant Savery created several paintings of the dodo while working as a Hapsburg court painter in the 1620s. One became the most universally accepted portrayal of the bird and influenced the dodo character in the book Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland.

CORNELIS SAFTLEVEN
C. 1638

The last watercolor from life

Twenty four years before extinction, Dutch Old Master Cornelis Saftleven created what is probably the last illustration of a dodo drawn from life. This watercolor presented the bird in a kinder, more dignified way than other interpretations. Some historians believe this portrait reveals features of the Rodrigues Solitaire, a closely related bird from the neighboring island that disappeared from the world at around the same time.

AMODERNMAURITIAN SYMBOL

Appearing on stamps, currency, coat of arms, and souvenirs, the dodo remains a proud national symbol for Mauritius.

It is also a daily reminder of the complex history between the island’s native wildlife and those who arrived there.

Expressions

“DUMB AS A DODO’
[IDIOM][1800]

"Dumb as a dodo" is an idiom based on the misconception that the flightless bird was unintelligent because it wasn’t afraid of humans or being hunted. In reality, they were more trusting because they evolved in isolation on the small island of Mauritius.

NAME ORIGIN
[1500]

Portuguese explorers who encountered the bird in the 1500s likely called it doudo, meaning "foolish" or "stupid," and the Dutch settlers offered their own slight: dodoor, meaning "sluggard." As scientists continue to prove, the dodo wasn't dim or lazy; it was fearless. What we call the bird today is a fossil of that original misunderstanding.

“DEAD AS A DODO’
[IDIOM][1800]

To say something is "dead as a dodo" means it’s finished beyond all doubt or recovery. Idioms like this and “gone the way of the dodo” have enshrined the bird into everyday English as a symbol of obsolescence.

Alice’s Adventures In Wonderland (1865, Book)

The novel by Lewis Carrol brought the dodo to life in one of the story's earliest scenes where it presided over a "Caucus Race,” with no clear rules and no real winner. Carrol gave a moment of power to one of history's most mocked creatures; the dodo convinced Alice that the only prize she needed was one from her own pocket.

Alice In Wonderland(1951, Film)

The Disney adaptation layered upon Carrol’s character with comedic animation and voice acting. This incarnation of the dodo cleverly reframed and recast the dodo as a ship’s captain who confidently shouts nonsensical orders but stays in charge of the others and the island.

Alice in Wonderland(2010, Film)

Tim Burton brings the dodo to life as Uilleam, a wise elder dressed in a waistcoat and glasses who is among the first to recognise Alice as the true one foretold by prophecy. Instead of the bumbling, comic stereotype, this role presents the dodo as a loyal and respected advisor.

Ice Age(2002, Film)

When a regimented flock of dodos appeared in one of the film's most memorable comedic scenes, the dodo was introduced to a new generation. Zealously preparing and hoarding melons to forestall their own extinction, again, their nature was no match for newcomers.

DOUGH FOR THE D0-D0 (Short Film, 1949)

In the 1938 Looney Tunes short "Porky in Wackyland,” Porky Pig travels in search of the last dodo on earth for a prize of four sextillion dollars. When Porky discovers the dodo, a character named Yoyo, the clever bird plays many tricks on him — eventually revealing a whole hidden flock. The film was so popular that it was remade as a colorful adaptation, “Dough for the Do-Do,” in 1949. The dodo made its way into the National Film Registry when the story was added by the Library Congress in 2000.

Zoo Tycoon 2 (2004, Game)

The dodo is an adoptable animal in Zoo Tycoon 2 where players were required to provide a "Misty Spring" in its habitat to be comfortable. The bird had its own unique enrichment item called the “Dodo Rock” that was intended to replicate the gastroliths that real dodos would have used to digest fruit.
