SCALING AND ACCELERATING CONSERVATION

At Colossal, we’re making extinction a thing of the past by expanding the conservation toolkit and putting it in the hands of partners worldwide. Each breakthrough we make on our journey toward

de-extinction doesn’t just push the boundaries of innovation, it also unlocks new approaches to conserving and recovering species today.

A new model for global conservation

Complex issues require innovative solutions. Colossal’s Conservation Framework connects cutting-edge technology, genomic insights, genetic rescue, biotechnology and community-led rewilding into a continuous pathway, from discovery and protection to recovery and return. By linking these five pillars into one end-to-end system, we advance a modern model for safeguarding biodiversity and giving threatened species a real chance to rebound.

Yellowstone Wolf Project

Together with our partners, the Colossal Foundation is pioneering a revolutionary new way to monitor and protect wolves through AI-powered bioacoustics. We deployed one of the world’s most advanced large-scale acoustic monitoring grids to transform Yellowstone into a living soundscape. This AI-enabled network of "digital ears" listens day and night, capturing and decoding thousands of individual wolf howls. This yields unprecedented insights into how wolves communicate, pack dynamics, population trends and behavior, and helps us detect threats in real time. We’re creating a blueprint to protect wolves and support coexistence with the human communities that share this landscape.

Safeguarding Genetic Diversity

Colossal preserves the genetic foundations of biodiversity through high-quality reference genomes, advanced sequencing, and a global biobanking network. These resources protect irreplaceable diversity and guide conservation strategies that strengthen species for the future.

BioVaults: Building the modern-day Noah’s ark

building a global biovault

biovault 001

//////////

Extinction is accelerating faster than at any point in human history. For thousands of species, the world may lose the critical genetic material needed for recovery long before technology or conservation interventions can arrive. The Colossal BioVault was created to change that trajectory. The BioVault will be the world's largest distributed biobanking initiative.

biovault 002

//////////

In collaboration with our partners, the Colossal Foundation is building a globally distributed network designed to protect the primary materials needed to prevent extinction. We're using this next-generation system to collect, preserve, and share viable tissue samples from the planet's most imperiled species.

Modern biobanking faces mounting challenges—from sample degradation, high storage costs, and limited genetic diversity to fragmented standards, access barriers, and growing ethical and sustainability concerns. Colossal is reimagining biobanking by building resilient, distributed collections; prioritizing high-quality, future-ready samples; pairing biological materials with rich, interoperable data; and expanding access through conservation-first partnerships. By aligning cutting-edge biotechnology with ethical stewardship and long-term resilience, Colossal is creating a biobanking model designed not just to preserve life—but to protect its future.

biovault 003

//////////

We currently have biovaults in Dallas and Dubai, and recently acquired Viagen, an Austin-based company with decades of expertise in assisted reproduction, cryopreservation, and biobanking.

Ending the Elephant EEHV Crisis

EEHV 001

//////////

Elephant endotheliotropic herpesvirus (EEHV) is one of the deadliest threats facing Asian elephants, responsible for the majority of deaths among young elephants in human care and an increasing risk to wild populations. To help change that, we funded and accelerated the development of the world's first mRNA vaccine for EEHV.

We're already seeing promising results. In spring 2025, two vaccinated young elephants at the Cincinnati Zoo—Sanjay and Kabir—were naturally exposed to the virus. Both remained healthy and recovered fully, marking the first real-world evidence that the vaccine can prevent severe disease and save lives.

EEHV 002

//////////

In partnership with Dr. Paul Ling at Baylor College of Medicine, we applied technologies originally developed for mammoth de-extinction to identify key viral targets and design a vaccine capable of training elephants' immune systems to stop the virus before it becomes fatal. The same advances in elephant genomics, gene analysis, synthetic biology, and next-generation mRNA delivery that support our de-extinction efforts are now helping protect living species.

This work reflects our broader vision for conservation: using breakthrough biotechnology not only to bring back extinct species, but to prevent endangered species from disappearing in the first place. With Asian elephant populations declining for decades and EEHV responsible for up to half of all juvenile elephant deaths in managed care, this vaccine has the potential to transform the future of elephant conservation.

restore 001

//////////

The American Red Wolf is the world's most endangered canid. Very few exist in the wild, and the roughly 300 under managed care are descended from just 12 individuals. This lack of genetic diversity leaves the species highly vulnerable to disease, inbreeding and extinction.

The Colossal Foundation and its partners are pioneering a new form of genetic rescue by identifying and cloning "ghost wolves" from the American Gulf Coast. These unique canids carry the precious genomic legacy of the pre-extinction red wolf, preserving vital diversity that was thought to be lost forever

In 2025, we held a naming ceremony to honor the first cloned pup. Meet Neka Kayda ("Ghost Daughter"), named by the Karankawa Tribe of Texas and now recognized as the tribe's totem animal.

Reintroducing Elephant Herds Back to the Wild

EEHV 001

//////////

Elephants are deeply social herd animals that rely on family structures to survive, learn behaviors, and navigate the wild. Through our partnership with Elephant Havens, the only elephant orphanage in Botswana, we’re using AI, machine learning, behavioral analysis, and genomic sequencing to help orphaned elephants form stable “synthetic herds” and successfully reintegrate into the wild. By comparing the behaviors of orphaned and wild elephants, we’re building a deeper understanding of elephant social dynamics, calf development, and herd behavior while creating better long-term reintroduction strategies for orphaned elephants.

This work is also foundational for mammoth de-extinction. Future mammoths cannot be raised alone. Like elephants, they will need social structures, learned behaviors, and herd relationships to thrive. Studying how orphan elephants bond, communicate, and develop within synthetic herds gives us an important framework for how future mammoths could be raised and integrated into functioning social groups from birth.

Inside Colossal’s AI-powered mission to rewild orphan elephants

EEHV 002

//////////

At the same time, through collaborations with Save the Elephants, we’ve used drones, infrared imaging, AI, and machine learning to map elephant movements, migratory corridors, and herd behaviors in the wild. By analyzing large-scale video and behavioral datasets, we can better understand how elephants move through ecosystems, respond to environmental pressures, and interact socially across landscapes. These insights help conservationists better protect elephants from poaching, habitat loss, and human conflict, while also giving us a blueprint for how future mammoths may navigate the environments they return to.
