Fat-Tailed Dunnart

Native to Australia, the fat-tailed dunnart plays a crucial role in Colossal’s plan to bring the Tasmanian tiger back from extinction. Though modest in size, this species is surprisingly resilient, adapted to a wide range of habitats and climates. Importantly, the fat-tailed dunnart shares over 99% of its genome with the thylacine making the fat-tailed dunnart a suitable candidate for the groundbreaking task ahead.

GENotypeS & PhenotypeS

Core phenotypic differences are the visible traits that shape how an animal lives and survives, and are central to de-extinction efforts. By comparing extinct species with their closest living relatives, scientists can identify key adaptations and explore ways to bring those traits back.

This work not only helps recreate aspects of lost species, but also reveals opportunities to build resilience in living ones—strengthening their ability to adapt to changing environments.

Follow successful efforts underway with the Tasmanian Devil.

Australia’s fauna has been devastated by invasive predators introduced by European settlers, only recently exacerbated by forest fires killing nearly three billion animals. Today, Australia has the world’s highest mammal extinction rate and ranks 6th worst in the world for number of species listed as endangered or critically endangered.

With a vision to enhance biodiversity in Australia, conservationists have turned to the Tasmanian devil. In Tasmania, devils keep wallaby and possum populations in check and serve as the nation’s largest scavenger, rapidly removing carcasses that would otherwise provide food for feral cats and introduced rodents. On the mainland, devils could help suppress invasive foxes and cats—both through direct competition and by reducing the food sources that sustain them—creating space for native small mammals to recover. In parallel, it would enrich the soil, spread seeds, bury leaf litter, and minimize the buildup of flammable material, dampening the opportunities for forest fires. To date, twenty-six healthy Tasmanian devils have been reintroduced to mainland Australia.

The Committee—which consists of cultural, commercial, government and community leaders in Tasmania—meets quarterly to discuss the feasibility of, concerns over and needs of the public in regards to the safe and successful reintroduction of the Thylacine. Conversations center around updates from Colossal related to genomics research, biodiversity, environmental restoration and the sustained wellness of the Tasmanian community.

Visit
Tasmania page

As part of our partnership with Tasmania, Colossal has assembled the Tasmania Thylacine Advisory Committee, a board of qualified individuals with diverse backgrounds, years of expertise and direct involvement with the community itself. The Committee’s main objective is to provide input on Colossal’s local conservation efforts and aid in the development of a Thylacine rewilding program in Tasmania.
