[ EEHV EXPLAINED ]

What is EEHV? 

EEHV (Elephant Endotheliotropic Herpesvirus) is a deadly hemorrhagic disease that has devastated elephant populations for decades, killing otherwise healthy calves in a matter of days through rapid internal bleeding and organ failure. The virus is now considered one of the leading causes of death for young Asian elephants under managed care, with fatality rates reaching up to 80% in the most vulnerable calves.

Wild Asian elephant populations have declined by more than 50% over the last century, and fewer than 52,000 remain on Earth today. Every calf now matters to the survival of the species. Amid habitat loss, poaching, and human-elephant conflict, EEHV has become one of the deadliest threats facing the next generation of elephants.

After years of research alongside the world’s leading EEHV scientists, we developed the first vaccine designed to protect elephants from the virus, transforming breakthrough mammoth and elephant genetics into a real-world conservation tool to help save the next generation of elephants.

The first EEHV vaccine, created by Colossal.

Status:
being tested in elephants and confirmed resistance

We developed the first EEHV vaccine by applying technologies originally built for mammoth de-extinction to one of the deadliest threats facing elephants today. In partnership with Dr. Paul Ling, the world’s leading EEHV researcher at Baylor College of Medicine, we used advanced genomics, synthetic biology, and computational protein engineering to identify key viral targets and design a vaccine capable of training elephants’ immune systems to recognize and stop the virus before it turns fatal.

The same technologies we are developing to revive the woolly mammoth, including elephant genome sequencing, gene analysis, multiplex engineering, and next-generation mRNA delivery systems, became the foundation for our EEHV program. By mapping elephant immune responses and analyzing genetic susceptibility across elephant populations, we transformed de-extinction science into a real-world conservation tool designed to protect living species today.

This work represents our broader vision for conservation: using breakthrough biotechnology not only to bring back extinct species, but to prevent endangered species from disappearing in the first place.

Asian elephant populations have declined by more than 50% over the last century, and EEHV is responsible for up to half of all juvenile elephant deaths in managed care. By stopping one of the deadliest threats facing young elephants, this vaccine could save more elephants than all traditional conservation efforts combined.

Explore how mammoth de-extinction is driving breakthrough technologies for elephant conservation

The challenge of reviving an extinct species requires technologies powerful enough to transform conservation itself. The woolly mammoth project has accelerated scientific capabilities that directly support elephant survival—shifting conservation from reactive protection to proactive resilience.

AI-Powered Monitoring & Decision-Making More time acting, less time tracking

///////////////////////////////////////////////////
+

Traditional wildlife monitoring relies on intermittent observation and manual analysis. AI transforms this paradigm by continuously analyzing movement patterns, social behavior, stress signals, and health indicators at scale.

+

These real-time insights allow conservationists to intervene earlier, design safer rewilding strategies, and manage populations dynamically—turning data into action before crises unfold.

002

Source

Disease Resistance AND Preventive Genetics Build strength, reduce outbreaks

+

Historically, conservation has responded to disease only after outbreaks occur. Colossal’s gene editing and immunology research enables a preventative approach—reducing susceptibility and strengthening natural resistance before disease can spread.

+

In the case of Elephant Endotheliotropic Herpesvirus (EEHV), this work accelerates vaccine development, supports targeted interventions, and advances long-term strategies to lower disease-driven mortality—addressing one of the leading threats to juvenile elephant survival worldwide.

High-resolution genomic sequencing allows conservation to move beyond population counts and into genetic foresight. By applying population genomics at scale, Colossal helps conservationists move beyond population counts toward genetic foresight, identifying inbreeding, fragmentation, adaptive diversity,

+

and hidden vulnerabilities that shape long-term survival. These insights support smarter breeding strategies, translocation, and protection decisions that preserve evolutionary resilience across generations.

Stem Cell & Reproductive Innovation Advancing how we preserve and restore species

+

Conventional conservation is limited by reduced gene flow and shrinking populations. Advances in induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), biobanking, and reproductive biology—developed through de-extinction research—create entirely new conservation options.

+

These tools make it possible to preserve genetic material indefinitely, recover lost diversity, and support reproduction even when populations fall below natural recovery thresholds.

Pushing

Building healthier elephant herds

through AI and behavioral science

conservation forward

Through the Colossal Foundation, we’re continuing to invest in new ways to protect and conserve elephants for the long term.

Elephants are deeply social herd animals that rely on family structures to survive, learn behaviors, and navigate the wild. Through our partnership with Elephant Havens, the only elephant orphanage in Botswana, we’re using AI, machine learning, behavioral analysis, and genomic sequencing to help orphaned elephants form stable “synthetic herds” and successfully reintegrate into the wild. By comparing the behaviors of orphaned and wild elephants, we’re building a deeper understanding of elephant social dynamics, calf development, and herd behavior while creating better long-term reintroduction strategies for orphaned elephants.

Follow the work
at Elephant Havens
Elephant Conservation
Supporting Orphan Elephant Rewilding

Rescuing and rehabilitating orphaned elephants, and preparing them for successful reintegration into wild herds.

This work is also foundational for mammoth de-extinction.

Future mammoths cannot be raised alone. Like elephants, they will need social structures, learned behaviors, and herd relationships to thrive. Studying how orphan elephants bond, communicate, and develop within synthetic herds gives us an important framework for how future mammoths could be raised and integrated into functioning social groups from birth.

At the same time, through collaborations with Save the Elephants, we’ve used drones, infrared imaging, AI, and machine learning to map elephant movements, migratory corridors, and herd behaviors in the wild. By analyzing large-scale video and behavioral datasets, we can better understand how elephants move through ecosystems, respond to environmental pressures, and interact socially across landscapes. These insights help conservationists better protect elephants from poaching, habitat loss, and human conflict, while also giving us a blueprint for how future mammoths may navigate and adapt to the environments they return to.
