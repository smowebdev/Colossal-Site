"TAD= Topologically Associated Domain"

Reconstructing Traits Through 3D Genome Architecture

///////////////////////////////////////////////////

+

We developed a pipeline that integrates transcription factor binding site (TFBS) analysis with 3D chromatin architecture, specifically topologically associating domains (TADs), to investigate how mammoth genetic variants influence gene regulation through chromatin reorganization. Our computational analysis identifies mammoth-specific variants that alter CTCF binding sites. These changes can create or disrupt TAD boundaries, either forming new regulatory neighborhoods or merging previously separate ones. As a result, a small number of mammoth-specific variants may have substantial phenotypic effects by reshaping TAD structure and altering the regulation of many genes.

+

TADs are a structural unit of the genome that organize DNA into self-interacting regions that allow Enhancers, Promoters, and regulatory elements to interact efficiently.  TADs are formed by cohesion motor proteins that reel in DNA to create loops, growing until they encounter molecular "stop signs" called CTCF proteins. These proteins lock the DNA into organized, self-contained neighborhoods. A TAD can span hundreds of thousands to millions of base pairs, so that even if a gene and enhancer are far apart on the genome, the 3D loop structure can bring them close so they can interact to regulate gene expression.

Mammoth-Specific Regulatory Rewiring

Colossal used this approach to find mammoth-specific variants that alter CTCF binding and reorganize TAD boundaries. These changes can create, remove, or merge regulatory neighborhoods, reshaping enhancer–gene interactions.

Because a single TAD can regulate many genes, a small number of variants can drive large phenotypic effects, influencing complex traits such as body size, ear length, tail length, and hair growth. This work represents a shift from single-gene analysis to systems-level engineering of extinct traits.
