THE WORLD'S FIRST ELEPHANT INDUCED PLURIPOTENT STEM CELLS

A LIVING PLATFORM FOR THE FUTURE OF SPECIES RESTORATION
+++

Colossal achieved a major world first by deriving Asian elephant induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs)—the first stem cell line ever created for an elephant.

Using a two-step reprogramming approach that includes chemical priming, pluripotency factor induction, and partial TP53 pathway suppression, adult elephant cells were returned to an embryonic-like state.

This breakthrough provides scientists with a renewable, ethical platform to study mammoth traits, model elephant biology, repair genetic vulnerabilities, and support conservation efforts.

Because elephant stem cells share key characteristics with those of other large mammals, this platform has far-reaching implications for wildlife conservation, regenerative medicine, and our understanding of how large, long-lived animals grow, heal, and sustain their bodies.

PRECISION GENOME CHANGES IN ELEPHANT CELLS

/////////////////////////////////////

+
Mammoth de-extinction requires coordinated gene editing across the elephant genome at an unprecedented scale. To achieve this, Colossal is developing multiplex gene-editing workflows capable of introducing hundreds to thousands of edits simultaneously while preserving cell health and genome stability.

Our mammoth team has already achieved more than 100 edits in a single experiment—the highest level of multiplex editing reported to date.

+
These capabilities are enabled by precision genome editing tools that allow targeted introduction of mammoth variants without creating damaging double-strand DNA breaks. Precision editing shortens experimental timelines, minimizes cellular stress, and supports stable large-scale genome rewriting.

As shown to the right, we begin by editing pools of cells, and then perform monoclonal sorts and sequence each cell colony separately. This allows us to learn the exact edited DNA sequence of each cell that we might use to make a mammoth.

The Longest Ancient DNA Sequences Ever Recovered from a Woolly Mammoth

Colossal scientists are collaborating with academic partners and our science advisors to develop the most accurately reconstructed mammoth genome to date and to expand our understanding of how genetically diverse mammoths were during the Ice Ages. Working with our advisors Love Dalén and Tom van der Valk, we recovered and analyzed the longest ancient DNA sequences ever isolated from a preserved woolly mammoth. In a separate study, we analyzed high coverage ancient genomes from 47 mammoths—some genomes exceeding 30× sequencing coverage. Together, these to studies push the boundaries of ancient DNA science and enable population-level analyses, regulatory genomics, and precise identification of mammoth-specific variants. The result is an unprecedentedly detailed genetic blueprint that underpins de-extinction science.

ABOUT

The crisis of biodiversity loss in the anthropogenic era requires new tools for studying non-model organisms. Elephants, for example, are both an endangered species and excellent models studying complex phenotypes like size, social behavior, and longevity, but they remain severely understudied. Here we report the first derivation of elephant [Elephas maximus] induced pluripotent stem cells [emiPSCs] achieved via a two-step process of chemical-media induction and colony selection, followed by overexpression of elephant transcription factors OCT4, SOX2, KLF4, MYC ± NANOG and LIN28A, and modulation of the TP53 pathway. Since the seminal discovery of reprogramming by Shinya Yamanaka, iPSCs from many species including the functionally extinct northern white rhinocerous have been reported, but emiPSCs have remained elusive. While for multiple species the reprogramming protocol was adopted with little changes compared to model organisms like mouse and human, our emiPSC protocol requires a longer timeline and inhibition of TP53 expansion genes that are hypothesized to confer unique cancer resistance in elephants. iPSCs unlock tremendous potential to explore cell fate determination, cell and tissue development, cell therapies, drug screening, disease modeling, cancer development, gametogenesis and beyond to further our understanding of this iconic megafauna. This study opens new frontiers in advanced non-model organism cellular models for genetic rescue and conservation.
