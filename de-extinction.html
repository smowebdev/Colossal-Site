De-extinction

[fuhngk-shuh-nl dee-uhk-STINK-shuhn] Noun

The process of generating an organism that both resembles and is genetically similar to an extinct species by resurrecting its lost lineage of core genes; engineering natural resistances; and enhancing adaptability that will allow it to thrive in today’s environment of climate change, dwindling resources, disease, and human interference.

Rebuilding Species for Today

De-Extinction of the Core Genes

Driving the phenotypes (physical characteristics) that have been lost to time in the lineage of the extinct species.

Can gene editing save endangered species? Increasingly, the answer is yes.

Breakthroughs from de-extinction don’t stay in the lab. They translate directly to species on the brink, from disease resistance to reproductive support and genetic rescue.

CRISPR lets scientists make precise DNA edits, and gene editing turns those into real-world solutions, like developing vaccines, boosting disease resistance, or improving fertility.

Conservation genomics shows where help is needed most by mapping genetic diversity.

Together, these tools don’t just protect species—they help them adapt and thrive.

De-extinction begins with recovering DNA from preserved remains—often bones, teeth, or tissue found in permafrost or dry environments.

How does DNA survive thousands of years?

Researchers isolate DNA in ultra-clean labs to prevent contamination. What they recover isn’t a full genome, but millions of fragmented sequences that can be read and analyzed.

Genome Reconstruction

Description

Recovered DNA fragments are computationally assembled into a working genome by aligning overlaps, filtering errors, and using related species as a reference to fill in gaps.

Q&A

3.

Can AI rebuild extinct DNA?

AI can rapidly align DNA fragments and predict missing sequences by comparing them to closely related species—but expert scientists are essential to guide the process, validate results, and ensure biological accuracy.

Precision Genome Editing

Index
+
Description

With a target genome defined, scientists edit living cells to express key traits—using tools like CRISPR to make precise changes to DNA. These edits can introduce characteristics such as disease resistance, climate adaptability, or physical traits, effectively translating a reconstructed genome into living biology.

Q&A

5.

What is CRISPR?

CRISPR is a gene-editing tool that lets scientists make precise changes to DNA, like molecular scissors guided to a specific spot in the genome to cut, remove, or replace genetic material.

CLSL

Embryology & Gestation

Index
+
Description

Edited DNA is used to create embryos that can develop into living organisms. Techniques like somatic cell nuclear transfer and induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) help generate viable embryos, which are carried by surrogate species or, increasingly, supported by emerging artificial reproductive technologies currently in development.
